## 2. Mission, Vision & Approach

- **Vision:** Empowered, self-reliant rural communities free from poverty.
- **Mission:** To promote rural well-being through expertise sharing, hands-on support, and long-term community commitment — enabling the most marginalized, especially rural women, to earn a dignified livelihood and take charge of their own lives.
- **Core strategic framework — "The Livelihood Triangle":**
  - **People** — building capabilities, skills, confidence, and entrepreneurial mindset (especially women & youth)
  - **Resources** — equitable access to and sustainable management of land, water, forests, and productive assets
  - **Institutions** — strengthening panchayats/VCDCs, SHG–VO–CLF networks, markets, and government systems
- **Focus groups:** Rural women, tribal communities (Bodo, Rabha, Garo, Adivasi/tea-tribe), and tea-garden workers.
- **Differentiators:** Continuous handholding (not one-time training); blending traditional knowledge (e.g., Dong-Bund water systems) with modern technology; convergence with government schemes (esp. MGNREGS); context-specific strategies tailored to land access.

2. Promote SHGs and build financial resilience
3. Develop entrepreneurs from tribal communities

5. Build climate-resilient farming capacity
6. Provide technical assistance to partner NGOs in the North-East and beyond

## 4. Thematic / Program Areas

- Rural livelihoods promotion & SHG/CLF/FPO strengthening
- Agriculture & horticulture (climate-resilient/rain-shelter vegetable farming, arecanut plantations, mustard/rapeseed cultivation, vermicompost production)
- Livestock-based livelihoods: poultry, piggery (incl. biosecurity work), goat rearing, duck farming, fisheries/aquaculture

- Women's empowerment & entrepreneurship development among tribal communities
- Technical assistance/capacity building to partner NGOs (in NE India and beyond)

## 5. Current & Recent Funders / Donors — Deep Dive (Amounts, Technology/Practices, Stakeholders)

Per KABIL's official website ("Funding Partners/Donors"), cross-checked against funder websites, BRLF, partner NGOs, and social-media project updates. **Caveat:** KABIL-specific grant amounts are rarely disclosed publicly; figures below are the best available — either the parent programme's scale (e.g., DWIMU, ABF Livelihoods Programme) or funder-published ceilings — clearly marked as such.

### 5.1 Axis Bank Foundation (ABF)
- **Project:** Udalguri Sustainable Livelihoods Project, implemented with **KABIL + Assam State Rural Livelihood Mission (ASRLM)**.
- **Duration:** 4 years (per KABIL's own data).
- **Problem framing (per ABF):** Udalguri block has high multidimensional poverty (SECC 2011); tea-tribe/Adibasi, Rabha, Garo and Bodo households rely on subsistence paddy + small-scale pig/goat rearing; despite good water sources, they lack **water-lifting facilities, capital, technical knowledge, and forward/backward market linkages**.
- **Technology / practices funded:** Productivity-enhancement inputs for paddy and allied crops; **water-lifting infrastructure** for irrigation; livestock (pig/goat) improvement; SHG-based capital deployment; market-linkage development; entrepreneurship development training.
- **Funding scale (context, not KABIL-specific):** ABF's overall Sustainable Livelihoods Programme operates across **21 states and 178 districts**, and has historically aimed to reach ~1 million people with a 50% income increase from baseline; nationally, the programme has formed 16,238 SHGs/JLGs with bank linkages worth ~₹86 Cr in borrowings and ~₹49 Cr in savings (these are ABF **national** figures, not the Udalguri-specific budget, which is not publicly disclosed).
- **Stakeholders involved:** Axis Bank Foundation (funder), KABIL (implementing NGO), ASRLM (state government livelihood mission — trains/deploys SHG members and channels funds), existing SHGs and community institutions in Udalguri.
- **Sources:** axisbankfoundation.org/partners/kabil.html; axisbankfoundation.org/sustainable-livelihood/sustainable-livelihood.html; csrbox.org (Axis Bank Sustainable Livelihood CSR Project page)

### 5.2 Azim Premji Foundation (APF)
- **Project A:** *"Strengthening the Livelihood of Rural Poor in Mazbat Block of Udalguri District"* — a KABIL-implemented project directly evaluated by APF (2-day field evaluation, 11–12 Feb 2026).
- **Project B (via BRLF/DWIMU convergence):** APF senior leadership jointly visited **DWIMU** project sites in Christianpara village, Udalguri Block, alongside BRLF leadership, PD/DRDA Udalguri, and block officials (BDP, BPM) and VCDC members — covering fishery, poultry, piggery, and MGNREGA-funded arecanut plantation units.
- **Technology/practices:** Livestock diversification (fishery, poultry, piggery units), MGNREGA-linked arecanut plantation development, household income tracking systems, livestock feed-management optimization.
- **Funding scale (context, not project-specific):** APF's institutional grant sizes generally "vary from a few lakh rupees to a few tens of crores," with tenures of 1–10 years (most 3–4 years) — per APF's own Grants page. The specific award amount to KABIL/Mazbat project is **not publicly disclosed**.
- **Stakeholders involved:** Azim Premji Foundation (funder/evaluator), KABIL (implementer in Mazbat Block), BRLF (convergence partner in DWIMU-linked visits), DRDA Udalguri, Block Development Project officials (BDP/BPM), VCDC members.
- **Sources:** instagram.com/p/DU0VdRiEi4o (KABIL); azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-organised/grants

### 5.3 SBI Foundation (SBIF)
KABIL's SBI Foundation-funded work spans (at least) **two distinct SBIF programmes**:

**(a) SBIF-CONSERW: Jaldhara** (Environment & Sustainability flagship program)
- **Focus:** Aquatic ecosystem conservation / water-resource conservation. In Udalguri, this funds work on repairing/reviving traditional **Dong systems** (community irrigation channels), water conservation, minor irrigation structures, green cover restoration, and diversified household livelihoods (backyard poultry, aquaculture) built around restored water sources.
- **Recorded activities:** One-day training on Hydrology & Watershed Management for project staff; training on Dong-system design, planning, operation & maintenance of community-based irrigation infrastructure (11 June 2026).
- **Funding scale (programme-wide, not KABIL-specific):** SBI Foundation's public "Call for Proposals: Aquatic Ecosystem Conservation" under SBIF CONSERW: Jaldhara lists an **award ceiling of INR 4,50,00,000 (₹4.5 crore)** per project — this is the general grant ceiling for the funding window, not a confirmed KABIL-specific award amount.
- **Stakeholders:** SBI Foundation (funder), KABIL (Udalguri implementation), **SeSTA** and **BRLF** (technical/convergence partners cited alongside KABIL in project posts), District-level government departments.

**(b) SBI Foundation "Gram Seva" Program**
- A separate, longer-running SBIF flagship programme (nationally active since ~2017, relaunched/expanded across 6 states in Oct 2022 by MD & CEO Sanjay Prakash) that funds rural infrastructure such as solar street lighting, alongside livelihood support. **Gramya Vikash Mancha (GVM)** — one of KABIL's own listed NGO/CSO partners — is confirmed to implement a Gram Seva project in Udalguri district (solar street lighting, launched with SBI Foundation). KABIL's own social posts reference "SBIF" recruitment of Community Resource Persons at its Udalguri office, suggesting KABIL is also a Gram-Seva-linked or SBIF-funded implementer in the same district, though the exact project name/scope distinct from Jaldhara is not fully disclosed publicly.
- **Sources:** sbifoundation.in/conserw; developmentaid.org (SBIF CONSERW: Jaldhara call for proposals, award ceiling ₹4.5 Cr); linkedin.com/company/kabilindia (Dong-system/Hydrology training posts); aninews.in / theprint.in / livemint.com (SBI Gram Seva launch, Sanjay Prakash); facebook.com/SBIFoundationIndia (World Elephant Day partnership post referencing Baksa/Udalguri human-elephant-conflict work, technology + community-centred model)

## 6. The DWIMU Mega-Project — Key Convergence Vehicle Linking Several Funders/Partners

Several of KABIL's funder relationships (APF, BRLF, SBIF/GVM) connect through a single large state-government convergence programme called **DWIMU**, which is important context for "who is involved and how much":

- **Full name:** **D**iversification **W**ith **I**ntegrated **M**anagement for **U**pliftment of people's lives (DWIMU)
- **Commissioning body:** Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR) Government / Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC) — invited BRLF's involvement in Jan 2021 at request of CEM Shri Pramod Boro
- **Lead convener:** **Bharat Rural Livelihoods Foundation (BRLF)** — formal MoU with BTR Government signed June 2022 (4-year term, extendable); a further MoU was signed between **ASRLM and BRLF** on 13 June 2025 specifically for DWIMU
- **Duration:** April 2023 – March 2028
- **Scale:** 40,000 ST/Adivasi/vulnerable households, across **56 VCDCs, 271 villages, 7 blocks, 3 districts** of the Bodoland Territorial Region (Assam) — KABIL implements in the **Udalguri and Mazbat blocks** specifically
- **Implementing CSOs:** Five Civil Society Organisations jointly implement DWIMU across BTR, including **KABIL** and **Gramya Vikash Mancha (GVM)** (both confirmed); each block has a dedicated CSO team for thematic interventions
- **Financial scale (program-wide, from BRLF's own reporting, not KABIL's share alone):**
  - 3,760 MGNREGS schemes initiated, with a **sanctioning value of ₹59.07 Crore**
  - 13,116 households received departmental support, with **₹33.86 Cr leveraged** and **₹66.81 Cr sanctioned value**
  - 13,816 households trained on livelihood activities; 7,375 households already earning income from those activities
  - ~5 lakh hectares of un-irrigated land targeted for conversion to irrigated land via water-harvesting/soil-conservation structures
- **Technology/practices funded under DWIMU:** Non-Pesticide Management (NPM) agriculture, improved irrigation, soil-moisture conservation & water harvesting structures, plantation drives, piggery/goatery/backyard-poultry/fisheries livestock models, MGNREGA-linked infrastructure, Geo-ICT/GIS-based spatial planning for scheme siting, a "DWIMU Dashboard" for digital monitoring, and a Lab-to-Land/Land-to-Market non-farm livelihoods approach
- **Government/institutional stakeholders:** BTC/BTR Government, ASRLM, DRDA (district-level), VCDCs, Panchayati Raj-equivalent local bodies, plus the funding foundations (APF, SBI Foundation, others) that back individual CSOs' portions of the work
- **Sources:** brlf.in/programs-at-brlf/state-partnership-projects/bodoland-program; facebook.com/BRLF.India (DWIMU/ASRLM MoU post, 15 June 2025); linkedin.com/company/kabilindia (DWIMU field-visit posts); researchgate.net (Geo-ICT/DWIMU spatial planning paper — abstract only, full text paywalled)

## 10. Challenges (Analytical Assessment)

> **Note on methodology:** KABIL has not published its own "challenges/risks" statement. The points below are an **analytical assessment inferred from patterns in the available public evidence** (what is/isn't published, how the org is discovered online, what its partners say) — not a direct quote or admission from KABIL itself. Each point is grounded in evidence gathered during this research and cited accordingly.

### Fundraising
- **Concentrated funder base:** All four named funders (Axis Bank Foundation, Azim Premji Foundation, SBI Foundation, Tata Trusts) are large domestic CSR/philanthropic foundations — no evidence was found of individual/retail donor fundraising, crowdfunding, or a "Donate" mechanism on KABIL's own website (contrast with BDF, which has an active donate page and emergency flood appeal). This suggests **fundraising is largely institution-to-institution (CSR/foundation grants), not diversified across donor types** — a common single-point-of-failure risk in the NGO sector.
  **Source:** https://www.kabilindia.org (homepage funder list; no donation call-to-action found on the site during this research)
- **No independently published financials:** No audited financial statements, annual report, or income/expenditure statement could be located publicly for KABIL. As a registered **Society** (not a Section 8 Company), KABIL is not required to file the same standardized financials with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs that a company like BDF must — meaning KABIL also does not show up on corporate-data aggregators like Tracxn, which could reduce visibility/discoverability to funders doing due diligence via such platforms.
  **Source:** Absence confirmed via lack of results in aggregator search (tracxn.com) and no annual-report page found on kabilindia.org, contrasted with rcrc.in/give.do only providing registration (not financial) data.
- **Dependence on renewal of multi-year contracts:** Several of KABIL's engagements (e.g., the original Axis Bank Foundation project, described as "4-year project initiated in April 2018") appear to be **fixed-term**; continuity of funding depends on successful renewal/new grant cycles rather than a diversified, evergreen income base.
  **Source:** https://give.do/discover/3WF/kabil ("in its third year of a four-year project initiated in April 2018")

### Technology / Data
- **Reliance on partner-owned digital infrastructure:** The most sophisticated digital tools associated with KABIL's work — e.g., the **"DWIMU Dashboard"** for monitoring and **Geo-ICT/GIS-based spatial planning** — belong to the BRLF-led DWIMU consortium, not to KABIL's own organizational systems. No evidence was found of KABIL operating its own MIS, beneficiary database, or public data dashboard.
